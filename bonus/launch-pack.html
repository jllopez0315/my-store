Where to send them from, and how to collect emails

For a handful of customers, your normal email works: paste the addresses into Bcc so nobody sees anyone else's. Past a few dozen, an email platform is easier. It sends to everyone at once, handles unsubscribes for you, and keeps you out of the spam folder. All of this is optional. The emails above work from any inbox.

Platforms that work well

  • MailerLite. Free to start, simple, and the one Claude can connect to directly (more on that below). A good default.
  • Kit (used to be ConvertKit). Free to start, built for creators, clean editor.
  • Brevo. Free plan with a daily send limit, and it does text messages too.
  • Mailchimp. The one everyone has heard of. Fine, a little busier.

Whichever you pick, read its rules about what you sell before you sign up. Some platforms will not carry research compound stores, and finding out after you have imported your list is the wrong time. Keep the wording guide below in every email and you remove most of the risk.

Collecting your customers' emails

Your store already does this. Every checkout asks for an email, and the box "Keep me posted on restocks and sales" is ticked unless the customer unticks it. Each order lands in your admin with the email and whether they said yes.

  1. Open your admin and click Customers in the left menu. One row per buyer, repeat buyers merged.
  2. Leave the box "Leave out anyone who opted out of emails" ticked. It is on by default. Anyone who unticked the box at checkout is left out of what you copy.
  3. Press "Copy emails" for a comma-separated list you can paste anywhere, or "Download CSV" for a spreadsheet with name, email, and order history.

Paste it into your platform. In MailerLite: Subscribers, Add subscribers, then either paste the copied list into "Copy and paste" or upload the CSV under "Import from file." Kit, Brevo, and Mailchimp all have the same two choices under Subscribers or Audience. Repeat this every few weeks, or whenever you have new buyers. The platform skips anyone already on the list.

The Restocks page keeps its own "Notify me" list. Those people asked about one item, not your newsletter, so the store emails them once when it is back and leaves them off this list.

Optional: let Claude send the campaign for you

On a paid Claude plan you can connect MailerLite to Claude under Settings, Connectors. Once it is connected, you never have to open MailerLite to send an email.

  1. In Claude, open Settings, then Connectors, find MailerLite, and press Connect. Sign in to MailerLite when it asks. Once.
  2. Paste the email you want to send, brackets filled in, and tell Claude what to do with it.
  3. Read the draft Claude shows you before it sends. It will ask.

Hashtags: a short note

Hashtags are the fastest way to say the wrong thing. A tag that hints at human use puts a post, and sometimes an account, at risk. Skip any tag that names a body goal, a feeling, a condition, or a fitness outcome, even a popular one.

Safe choices: your store name as one word, like #[STORENAME], and nothing else. Or no hashtags at all. Both are fine. Your existing customers find you by name, not by tag.

Wording guide: allowed and never

Everything on your store is sold for research use only. The words you post have to match. This one page keeps you on the right side of that line when you write your own posts and emails. Read it once. Keep it open while you write.

Allowed

  • research compounds
  • research use only
  • lab reports, certificate of analysis
  • purity tested, verified lab reports
  • lot number, new lot, restock
  • in stock, ships from [CITY/STATE]
  • store now open
  • entry gate, sign in with a code
  • how to order
  • product name, size, and price

Never

  • any word about what a compound does to a body
  • weight, fat, muscle, skin, sleep, mood, energy, or age
  • healing, repair, or anything about feeling better
  • results, before and after, or a customer saying what happened to them
  • dosing, how much, how often, or any plan for a person
  • the words take, use on, or benefits
  • pictures of syringes, needles, or injection
  • calling the store compliant, legal, or approved
  • health claims of any kind, even in a reply

The quick test. Read your post out loud. If a stranger could hear it and think "that's for people," rewrite it. Name the compound, the size, the lot, and the lab report. Stop there.

Replies count too. A customer may ask what something does. The safe answer is one line: "Everything here is for research use only. I can't speak to anything else." Then move on.
