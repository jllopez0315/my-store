The Store Kit, bonus

Canva Social Kit

Ten ready-made graphics for opening your store, written for research use only. Open one in Canva, put it in your colors and font, fill in the brackets, and post. Seven short steps below.

What's in the kit

Every graphic is laid out with placeholder text in square brackets. The wording on each one is done and matches the launch pack. You change the brackets, the colors, and the font if you like, and nothing else, unless you want to.

1. Open a template

Each graphic is its own Canva template, at its own size. A free Canva account is enough.

  1. Click "Open in Canva" beside the graphic you want. If you're not signed in, Canva asks you to sign in or make a free account.
  2. Press "Use template." Canva makes a copy that belongs to you. The original stays untouched, so you can come back for a fresh copy any time.
  3. Rename your copy. Click the title at the top and give it your store's name. Keep all ten copies in one folder in your Canva so they're easy to find.

2. Put it in your colors

The kit ships in a neutral palette so it looks finished before you touch it. Two colors do the work.

Navy, the background
Brass, the accent
Off-white, the text
  1. Click the navy background. A color square appears at the top left of the editor. Click it.
  2. Pick your brand color, or type its hex code (the same one in your store's settings). Canva offers "Change all." Press it. Every navy on the page becomes your color.
  3. Do the same for brass. Click the thin brass line, pick your accent color, press "Change all."

Keep the text off-white on a dark background, or dark on a light one. If your brand background is light, change the text color last, the same way, with "Change all."

Your logo: click Uploads on the left, upload it, drag it onto the page, and place it in a corner. Keep it small. The words are the point.

3. Change the font

  1. Press Cmd+A (Ctrl+A on Windows) to select everything on the page.
  2. Open the font box at the top left and pick your font. The whole page changes at once.
  3. Look once. If a line wraps or runs off the edge, click that text and lower its size a step.

The kit ships in one clean sans-serif, so leaving the font as it is also looks right.

4. Fill in the brackets

Double-click any text to edit it. Replace [STORE NAME], [STORE LINK], [COMPOUND], [SIZE], [PRICE], and [LOT NUMBER] with your own. Leave the lines that say "Research use only" exactly as they are. They're there on purpose.

Posting a new lot every few weeks? Make a copy of your New lot in file each time instead of editing the first one, so you keep a clean master.

Vial labels and Kit of ten: the label on each vial is four text boxes, store name, compound, size, and the research line. On the kit, press Cmd+F (Ctrl+F on Windows) for Find and replace, type [COMPOUND], type your compound, and press Replace all: all five labels change at once. Your store's own product pictures are printed by the store itself, so these two are for social posts and mockups, not for the shop.

5. Want pictures? Make them with AI

The templates are words on color on purpose: they post cleanly and nothing in them needs explaining. If you'd rather have an image, make one with AI and drop it in. Use the sizes from the table so it fits.

  1. In Canva: click Apps on the left, open the AI image maker (Canva calls it Magic Media), type what you want, and pick the size that matches the template. Free accounts get a few images a month; ChatGPT, Gemini, and others make them too. If you use one of those, ask for the exact pixel size in the table, download the picture, and upload it under Uploads.
  2. Drag it onto the page. To put it behind the words, right-click it and choose "Set image as background," then click the words and make sure they still read. A darker image, or one with a plain area where the words sit, works best. On the Product card, drop it into the outlined square.
  3. Keep the same rules. No people, no bodies, no pills, no syringes, nothing being injected or taken, and nothing that hints at an effect. Abstract shapes, lab glassware with no label, a plain vial, textures, and clean still life are all fine. Keep "Research use only" on the page.

A prompt that works: "Minimal, moody product still life: a single plain glass vial on a dark surface, soft light from one side, lots of empty space, no label, no text, no people. 1080 by 1080 pixels." Swap the colors or the object to match your brand.

Want a whole new post at the same size? In Canva, press Create a design, then Custom size, and type the numbers from the table.

6. Your vial as an AI reference

The Vial labels and Kit of ten templates do double duty. Once they carry your label, they become a reference picture you can hand to an AI, so every image or video it makes shows your vial, not a random one. Two ways to get that picture.

  1. From Canva. Fill in the vial label with your store name, compound, and size, set your colors, then Share, Download, PNG. That file is your reference.
  2. From your own store. Your shop already draws every vial and kit with your label on it. Open a product page, make the vial as large as you can on screen, and take a screenshot (Cmd+Shift+4 on a Mac, Windows+Shift+S on Windows). Crop it close. That works just as well, and it matches the store exactly.
  3. Attach it, then ask. In ChatGPT, start a new chat, attach the picture, and describe the scene you want. In Gemini, do the same for a still image, or ask for a short video clip from the picture. In Canva's own image maker, you can also upload the picture and ask for variations. Say "keep the vial and its label exactly as in the attached picture" and the result stays yours.

A prompt that works: "Use the attached vial exactly as it is, label and all. Place it on a dark stone surface with soft light from one side, a little mist, lots of empty space. No people, no hands, no text. 1080 by 1080 pixels." For video in Gemini: "Slow push-in on the attached vial on a dark surface, soft light, no people, no text, five seconds."

Same rules as always. No people, no bodies, no pills, no syringes, nothing being used. Check the label is still readable in the result, and keep "Research use only" on the post. The AI will sometimes redraw your label with made-up words: look before you post.

7. Post it

Click Share at the top right, then Download. PNG is the right file type for social and for the email header. For the story, add your link sticker inside the outlined box when you post it.

Keep the wording safe

Every graphic in this kit uses research use only wording. If you add text of your own, check it against the wording guide first. It's one page, and it's in your launch pack.

Open the wording guide

The short version: name the compound, the size, the lot, and the lab report. Never say what a compound does to a body. No body imagery in the pictures. No hashtags that hint at human use.
