What's in this folder

Two folders and a few files. The store folder is what your buyers see. The admin folder is your private back office. Everything else is for you and your AI assistant.

The setup steps, in order

These are the steps we do in the sessions. They're written out here so you can follow along, catch up, or do them again for a second store. Do them in order. Each one builds on the last.

1

Put the store online

Two ways to do this. Both use the same files and both send order emails. Pick one. You can switch from A to B later without starting over.

Path A: drag and drop. Recommended for most owners. No GitHub. You drag the store folder into Cloudflare. To publish a change later, you drag it again. Two minutes, every time.

Path B: GitHub connected. For owners who want Claude to publish by itself when they say "publish." Cloudflare watches your GitHub repo and goes live on every push. A few more clicks today, fewer clicks every day after.

Path A: drag and drop
  1. Log in at dash.cloudflare.com. In the left menu click Workers & Pages.
  2. Click Create, then the Pages tab, then Upload assets.
  3. Project name: your store's name, lowercase, no spaces (this becomes your free address, like my-store.pages.dev). Click Create project.
  4. Open your kit folder in Finder or File Explorer. Drag the store folder (the folder itself, not its contents) onto the upload box. Wait for the file list to finish.
  5. Click Deploy site. Click the .pages.dev link. Your store is online with the placeholder content.

To publish a change later on Path A: Cloudflare, Workers & Pages, your store project, Create deployment, drag the store folder in again, Save and Deploy. Live in about a minute.

Path B: GitHub connected
  1. Open your kit folder in VS Code. Open the Claude Code panel. Say this to Claude:
Create a private GitHub repo called my-store under my account and push this whole folder to it. Don't publish anything else.
  1. Claude may ask you to sign in to GitHub in the browser. Do that, come back, and let it finish. When it's done, github.com shows your repo with the store and admin folders in it. Confirm the repo says Private.
  2. Log in at dash.cloudflare.com. Workers & Pages, Create, Pages tab, Connect to Git.
  3. Connect your GitHub account when asked, pick your my-store repo, and click Begin setup.
  4. On the build settings screen:
    • Project name: your store's name, lowercase, no spaces
    • Production branch: main
    • Framework preset: None
    • Build command: leave empty
    • Build output directory: /
    • Open Root directory (advanced) and type store
  5. Click Save and Deploy. Wait for the green check. Click the .pages.dev link. Your store is online with the placeholder content.

To publish a change later on Path B: say "publish" to Claude. It pushes, Cloudflare rebuilds, live in about a minute.

Whichever path you picked, tell Claude. Open CLAUDE.md, find the Publishing lines, and delete the path you're not using. That's how Claude knows what "publish" means for you.

2

Set the environment variables

The order code (_worker.js) needs a few keys to send email and to save orders into your admin. They live in Cloudflare, never in your files. You can set the email ones now and come back for the Supabase ones after Step 3.

Part A: get your Resend key
  1. Log in at resend.com. In the left menu click Domains, then Add Domain. Type your domain. Resend shows you a few DNS records to add where your domain is registered. If your domain is at Cloudflare, add them under your domain's DNS page. Wait for Resend to show Verified. (This is what lets receipts reach your buyers. Without a verified domain, Resend only delivers to your own address.)
  2. Click API Keys, then Create API Key. Name it "store orders," permission Sending access. Copy the key now. Resend only shows it once.
Part B: paste the keys into Cloudflare
  1. In Cloudflare, open Workers & Pages, click your store project, then Settings, then Environment variables (under Variables and Secrets on some screens).
  2. Click Add variable for each line below. Choose Production. Mark the keys as Secret (encrypt) where offered.
    • RESEND_API_KEY: the key you just copied
    • ORDER_TO: the email address where you want order alerts
    • ORDER_FROM (optional): the address receipts come from, like orders@yourdomain.com. Must be on the domain you verified in Resend. Leave it out until the domain is verified.
    • SUPABASE_URL and SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY (optional, after Step 3): these let the function save orders into your admin. Where to find them is in Step 4.
    • SUPABASE_ANON_KEY (optional, after Step 3): the same anon public key you put in settings.js. With it, your store keeps a safety copy of your saved look at the edge, so visitors see your chosen look even on a day your database is asleep. Together with the two above and RESEND_API_KEY, it also lets your admin send the "it's back in stock" emails to shoppers who asked for one.
    • AUTH_SECRET: any 32 or more random characters. To make one, open your password manager, generate a password 40 characters long, and paste it here. With it set, buyers must sign in with a six-digit code sent to their email before the checkout form appears, and every order alert tells you the buyer's email was verified. Without it, orders are accepted without sign-in.
  3. Click Save. New variables only take effect after a fresh deploy. Path A: Create deployment and drag the store folder in again. Path B: Deployments, three-dot menu on the latest one, Retry deployment.

Never paste these keys into Claude, into a file, or into an email. Cloudflare is the only place they live. If one ever leaks, delete it in Resend or Supabase and make a new one.

3

Create the database with setup.sql

Your orders, inventory, customers, and admin login live in a Supabase project you own. One script creates everything.

Where to click
  1. Log in at supabase.com. Click New project.
  2. Name it after your store. Set a strong Database password and save it somewhere safe (you rarely need it again, but you can't recover it). Pick the region closest to you. Click Create new project. Wait a minute or two while it builds.
  3. In the left menu click SQL Editor, then New query.
  4. Open admin/setup.sql from your kit folder in VS Code. Select all, copy, and paste it into the query box.
  5. Click Run. You want "Success. No rows returned" or similar. If you see red, copy the error and ask Dr. Johnna live in the session. Don't run it twice.

The script creates four tables: your orders and expenses, your inventory, store_settings, a single row that holds what you choose on the admin's Look & layout screen (name, tagline, logo, dark or light, layout, fonts, colors, background, the three facts, the vial label), and restock_notify, the shoppers waiting for a back-in-stock email. It also creates a storage bucket named labels for your uploads, and two read-only lookups the store uses: your live counts (so the shop shows what is really on the shelf) and the "notify me" sign-up.

Then connect the store to it

For the store to read that row, settings.js needs two values from the same place you copy them for the admin (Project Settings, then API): the Project URL and the anon public key. They go in the sync block. The anon key is safe to publish; it can only read that one row.

Fill in the sync block in settings.js: supabaseUrl is [PROJECT URL] and anonKey is [ANON KEY]. Run the syntax check and show me the preview.

Say "publish" and from then on the Look & layout screen in your admin changes the live store within seconds: Preview shows a change on your store without saving, Save makes it live, Reset to defaults puts the stock look back in the form. The Header section takes your logo (one for each look), the Home page background section takes your own picture or a short silent video loop, the Restocks page switch adds a page that lists everything out of stock, and the Vial label section prints your name, colors, logo, or a full label design on every vial picture, all with uploads. The values in settings.js stay as the fallback.

Free Supabase projects go to sleep after about a week with no activity. If your admin ever says it can't connect, log in to supabase.com and click Restore on the project. Takes about 30 seconds.

4

Create the admin login and connect the admin

Part A: your login
  1. In Supabase, left menu, click Authentication, then Users.
  2. Click Add user, then Create new user. Enter your email and a strong password. Tick Auto Confirm User. Click Create user.
Part B: the three values the admin needs
  1. In Supabase, click the gear icon (Project Settings), then API (called Data API on some screens).
  2. Copy the Project URL. Copy the anon public key. (You'll also see a service_role key marked secret. That one goes into Cloudflare as SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY in Step 2. It never goes into any file.)
  3. Back in VS Code, say this to Claude:
Open admin/index.html and fill in ADMIN_CONFIG. storeName is [YOUR STORE NAME]. storeUrl is [YOUR STORE'S WEB ADDRESS]. supabaseUrl is [paste the Project URL]. supabaseAnonKey is [paste the anon public key]. Then run the pre-publish check and show me.
Part C: put the admin online

Same idea as Step 1, with the admin folder, as its own project.

  1. Path A: Cloudflare, Workers & Pages, Create, Pages, Upload assets. Project name my-store-admin. Drag the admin folder in. Deploy site.
  2. Path B: Create, Pages, Connect to Git, the same my-store repo. Project name my-store-admin. Preset None, build command empty, output /, Root directory (advanced) admin. Save and Deploy.
  3. Open the .pages.dev link. Log in with the user you just created.

The admin address is private because it needs a login, not because it's hidden. Don't share the link publicly anyway. If you'd rather not host it, you can open admin/index.html straight from your computer. Hosting it means you can check orders from your phone.

6

Place a test order

This proves the whole chain: gate, cart, order code, both emails, and the admin.

  1. Make sure SUPABASE_URL, SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY, and SUPABASE_ANON_KEY are set in Cloudflare (Step 2, Part B) and you deployed again after adding them.
  2. Open your live store on your phone. Go through the gate. Add one item. Check out using a second email address you control as the buyer.
  3. Check the inbox at ORDER_TO. You should have an order alert.
  4. Check the buyer inbox. You should have a receipt with your payment instructions.
  5. Open your admin. The order should be there, already recorded as a Paid sale, and the stock count for that item is one lower than it was. Set it to Cancelled so it doesn't count in your reports. Cancelling puts the stock back.

How order statuses work from here: every website order lands in your admin as Paid and lowers stock on its own. You change a status only if the money never arrives (set it to Awaiting payment or Cancelled) or when you ship it (set it to Shipped). There's nothing to click when a normal order comes in.

No alert email? Check Cloudflare: your project, Deployments, the latest one, then the logs. A missing or misspelled variable is the usual cause. On Path A, also confirm _worker.js is in the file list of the deployment (you dragged the store folder itself, not its contents). No buyer receipt but you got the alert? Your Resend domain isn't verified yet. No order in the admin? The two Supabase variables are missing or you didn't deploy again after adding them.

7

Connect your domain and set up a branded email address

We do this in Session 3. The domain makes your store yours at an address you own. The branded address (like orders@yourstore.com) is what your buyers' receipts come from, and what they reply to.

Part A: point the domain at your store
  1. In Cloudflare, open your store project, click Custom domains, then Set up a custom domain.
  2. Type your domain (for example yourstore.com) and click Continue.
  3. If your domain is registered at Cloudflare, it adds the records for you. Click Activate domain.
  4. If your domain is somewhere else (Namecheap, GoDaddy), Cloudflare shows a CNAME record. Log in at your registrar, open DNS settings, add that record exactly as shown, and save. Come back and wait for Active. This can take a few minutes or a few hours.
  5. Repeat for www.yourstore.com if you want both to work.
  6. For the admin, do the same on the admin project with a subdomain like admin.yourstore.com.
  7. Say this to Claude so your receipts and legal pages show the right address:
My store is now at https://yourstore.com. Update every place the .pages.dev address appears and show me.
Part B: a branded email address at your domain
  1. Sending: your domain must show Verified in Resend (Step 2, Part A). The DNS records Resend gives you go wherever your domain's DNS lives, Cloudflare or your registrar. Once it's verified, set ORDER_FROM in Cloudflare to orders@yourstore.com and deploy again. Receipts now come from your brand, not a generic address.
  2. Receiving: buyers will reply to that address, so it needs to reach you. If your domain's DNS is on Cloudflare, open the domain, click Email, then Email Routing, and add a rule that forwards orders@yourstore.com to the inbox you check. Free. If your DNS is elsewhere, your registrar has an email forwarding setting that does the same job.
  3. Send yourself one email at the new address and confirm it lands.

Running your store with Claude

Your store is a small set of files. You never edit them by hand. You tell Claude what you want in plain English, Claude makes the change and shows you, and you say "publish." That's the whole job. Claude does the work of the dashboard other platforms charge monthly for.

The daily routine

  1. Open VS Code. Open your store folder.
  2. Open the Claude Code panel.
  3. Say what you want.
  4. Claude makes the change, runs the syntax check, opens a preview, and tells you what to look at.
  5. Look at the preview. If it's right, say publish.
  6. Path B: Claude pushes and it's live about a minute later. Path A: Claude tells you the folder is ready. You open Cloudflare, your store project, Create deployment, drag the store folder in, and it's live about a minute later.

Buyers sign in with a code sent to their email before they can order (no passwords, nothing for you to manage), and every order alert says whether that email was verified. Orders, inventory counts, customers, and your store's look are in your admin, in your browser. Look changes (name, tagline, dark or light, layout, fonts, colors, the three facts): admin, Look & layout, Preview to check it on the store, Save to make it live, Reset to defaults to start over. Products and prices, shipping, payment, the gate wording, and page copy: Claude, as below.

Publish

Four habits that keep you safe

  1. Look before you publish. Claude shows you the preview first. Read it. A typo in a price is a real price the moment it goes live.
  2. One change at a time. "Change this price," then "publish," beats a list of twelve things. Easier to check, easier to undo.
  3. Never paste passwords or keys into Claude. Payment handles that already show on your store are fine. Logins and API keys are not.
  4. When Claude gets something wrong twice, don't just correct it. Say: "Add that to CLAUDE.md so you remember it next time." That one habit is how your Claude gets better every week.

A good first week

  • Day 1: "Read CLAUDE.md and tell me in plain English what my rules are." Just listen.
  • Day 2: Change one price. Publish. Change it back. Publish again.
  • Day 3: Mark one item out of stock, then back in.
  • Day 4: Ask for something the rules forbid ("add a line saying this helps with recovery"). Watch Claude flag it. That's the moment you trust the guardrails.
  • Day 5: "Review my store like a buyer."

Updating the kit later

When Dr. Johnna releases a new version, you download the new zip. Your store stays where it is. Same site, same address, same accounts. You never make a new site for an update.

Your own files are the ones you filled in: store/settings.js, store/data.js, the pictures in store/img/, the lab reports in store/coa/, the ADMIN_CONFIG block in admin/index.html, and your edits to store/legal.html. Everything else is kit code.

The update is: copy the new kit files over the old ones, except those owner files, then publish again (drag the folder in again on Path A, or push on Path B). With Claude Code it's one prompt:

If the version note says the database changed, there will be a small SQL file in the download to run once in your Supabase SQL Editor, the same way you ran setup.sql. The note will say so, and which file.

Research use only

The lines your store never crosses

  • Research use only, everywhere. On the gate, on every product, in every email. The kit says it. Your wording keeps saying it.
  • No dosing, no protocols, no usage guides. Not on the site, not in a session, not in a reply to a buyer.
  • No health claims. No benefits, results, before-and-after, "treats," "heals," "weight loss," "anti-aging," or anything that implies human or animal use. Product names and sizes only.
  • Never call your store "compliant" or "legal." No tool can make a business compliant, and this one doesn't claim to. That word belongs to your attorney, not your website.
  • The store never takes card numbers. Payment handles and a hosted checkout link only. That's by design and it's also a condition of the free hosting plan.
  • Your attorney reviews before you open. The legal pages are drafts. Your products, your wording, your buyers, and the laws that apply to you are your responsibility.

Claude is told all of this in CLAUDE.md. When it flags something you asked for, listen. That flag is doing its job.
